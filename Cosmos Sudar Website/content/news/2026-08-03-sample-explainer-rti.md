Most people who give up on the Right to Information Act give up at the same place: the silence after the first application. That silence is not the end of the process. It is a specific stage of it, with a specific remedy, and the remedy is free.

## Filing it

1. **Write it on plain paper.** There is no official form for Tamil Nadu. A sheet of paper works.
2. **Address it to the Public Information Officer** of the office that holds the record — the panchayat, the municipality, the highways division, whichever one would physically have the file.
3. **Ask for records, not opinions.** "Why is the road bad?" gets you nothing. "Copy of the work order, estimate, and completion certificate for road work in survey number X between January 2024 and December 2025" gets you a file.
4. **Pay ₹10.** By court fee stamp, postal order, or cash against receipt. If you hold a BPL card, you pay nothing.
5. **Send it by registered post with acknowledgement due.** Keep the receipt. That receipt is what starts the clock.

## The clock

- The office has **30 days** to reply.
- If the information concerns someone's life or liberty: **48 hours**.
- If they say nothing at all by day 31, that counts in law as a **refusal**. This is the part most people do not know.

## The appeal nobody uses

Within 30 days of that deemed refusal, you write a **First Appeal** to the First Appellate Authority — usually the officer one rank above the PIO in the same office. It costs nothing. You attach a copy of your original application and the postal receipt, and you state one line: no reply was received within the statutory period.

A very large share of applications that go unanswered are answered within weeks of a first appeal landing, because the appeal creates a record that a senior officer has to close.

If the first appeal also fails, a **Second Appeal** goes to the Tamil Nadu Information Commission. That is slower — often a year or more — but it is also free, and the Commission can impose a penalty on the officer personally.

> The Act works less because officials fear the Commission, and more because they dislike paperwork that names them.

## What to keep

Keep everything: the application copy, the postal receipt, the acknowledgement card, every reply. If a story ever comes out of it, that paper trail is what makes it publishable.
